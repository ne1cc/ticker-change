Options Terminal Methodology & Mathematical Assumptions

1. Dollar Gamma Exposure (GEX) Formula

GEX_$1% = Γ × s × Contracts × 100 × Spot² × 0.01 / 1,000,000

Where Γ is Black-Scholes gamma, \(s\) is the dealer-positioning sign convention, and Spot² scales contract delta hedging requirements to dollar market cap per 1% underlying shift.

2. 0DTE Volume Weighting

Because the OCC publishes official open interest overnight, 0DTE options typically display stale zero open interest during the trading day despite massive intraday volume. For any contract where \(DTE = 0\), contract weight defaults to same-day volume rather than stale OI.

3. Dealer Sign Conventions

  • Naive Convention: Assumes institutional and retail clients are net buyers of puts and net sellers/overwriters of calls (\(s_{call} = +1, s_{put} = -1\)).
  • Short Wings Convention: Assumes market makers are short out-of-the-money options (puts below spot, calls above spot) to satisfy customer tail hedges (\(s_{OTM} = -1, s_{ITM} = +1\)).

4. Continuous Grid-Revalued Gamma Flip

Rather than holding gamma static, the zero-gamma flip level is determined by dynamically re-evaluating Black-Scholes gamma across a 201-point spot price grid from \(0.5 \times Spot\) to \(1.5 \times Spot\) and interpolating the zero crossing.

5. Yang-Zhang (1998) Realized Volatility

A minimum-variance, drift-independent estimator combining overnight jumps, continuous open-to-close drift, and Rogers-Satchell high-low intraday components over 30-day and 90-day windows.

6. Empirical Touch-and-Reversal Event Study

Evaluates whether underlying price action historically respects structural levels: Put Wall support (reversal upwards), Call Wall resistance (reversal downwards), and Gamma Flip crossing (volatility expansion). Benchmarked against a 25-iteration Monte Carlo random price level baseline with one-tailed z-test significance testing (p < 0.05).
